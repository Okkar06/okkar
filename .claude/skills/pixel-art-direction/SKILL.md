# Pixel art direction — Okkar's Village

The village mixes Kenney's **Tiny Town** and **Tiny Dungeon** (CC0) with sprites generated by
`client/tools/build-art.mjs`. Everything must read as ONE art style.

## 1. One palette
- The only colours allowed live in `client/src/theme/palette.js`. They were sampled from the Kenney
  tilesets, so new art and Kenney art share colours.
- Generated sprites use palette **names** (`ink`, `woodLight`, `leaf` …), never raw hex.
- Phaser code that needs a colour (tints, particles, glows) imports `palette.js` too (`hex('water')`).
- Need a new colour? Add it to `palette.js` with a comment saying why. Keep the palette under ~40 colours.
- Third-party art that isn't Kenney's must be recoloured to the palette before use. If that isn't
  practical, draw the asset yourself in `build-art.mjs` instead.

## 2. One tile size and pixel scale
- Tiles are **16×16**. Sprites are drawn at 1× (no pre-scaled pixels, no half pixels).
- Multi-tile objects are multiples of 8 px where possible (8, 16, 24, 32 …).
- The camera zoom is always a whole number (`pickZoom`), and `pixelArt: true` keeps edges crisp.

## 3. Light and shading
- Light comes from the **top-left**. Highlights go on top/left edges, shade on bottom/right.
- 2–3 tones per material: highlight, base, shade (e.g. `woodLight`, `wood`, `woodDark`).
- No gradients or anti-aliasing inside sprites. Soft gradients are only allowed for **light/glow**
  textures created at runtime (window glow, lamp glow, fog).

## 4. Outlines
- Like Kenney's tiles, every object (furniture, props, characters, fences) has a **1 px `ink`
  outline** (`#3f2631`).
- Ground-level details that are part of the floor (grass tufts, flowers painted on grass, puddles,
  water sparkles, rug patterns) have **no** outline.
- Particles (rain, snow, smoke, petals) have no outline.

## 5. Shadows
- Every free-standing object casts a soft shadow: the shared `shadow` texture (dark ellipse,
  ~25% alpha), drawn under the object's feet and slightly to the **bottom-right** (light is top-left).
- Buildings and fences use the shadow already drawn in their tiles.

## 6. Readability first
- Silhouettes must be readable at 2× zoom on a phone.
- Interactable things get a hint: a sign, lantern, name plate or sparkle. Decoration must never look
  more "clickable" than the real interactables.
- Never place art over HTML prompts or panels; text readability beats atmosphere.

## Checklist before committing art
- [ ] Colours come only from `palette.js`
- [ ] 16 px grid, whole pixels, top-left light
- [ ] Outline rule followed (objects yes, ground details/particles no)
- [ ] Shadow under every free-standing object
- [ ] Credited in `CREDITS.md` (generated art: "made for this repo")
